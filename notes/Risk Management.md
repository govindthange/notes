# R/R Ratio

Win rate goes up as R/R goes down and vice versa.

If you focus on taking more profits then R/R will get compromised.

Example:

`Win Rate` = 90 <-- Say you want 90% of your trades to be successful.
`Risk to Reward` = 10 <-- To get 90% win rate you will have to expose yourself to all the possibilities that can get maximum win rate. By risking $10 to make as little as $1 would expose your positions enough to turn 90% of all trades into winners. But there is a problem!

`Total Trades Taken` = 100
`Winning Trades` = 90 <-- As 90% win rate.
`Losing Trades` = 10

Calculation as per 10:1 `R/R`

```
Total Profit = (90 * $1) - (10 * $10) = -100 <-- Get wrecked!
```

On the contrary, if your win rate is low but keep a reasonable R/R then you can still make decent profits in the long run.

# R/R Guidelines

[By Anish Singh Thakur](https://youtu.be/iXN9FqGOQWM?t=1863)

- One should never have R/R below 1:1.5
- Swing trades should always have R/R of 1:2. 1:3 is ideal for swing trades.

# Position of Strength

> Always trade from the position of strength. Do not get wrecked!

Before entering a trade most of the retail traders wait for a confirmation signal and then enter trade with a tight S.L. set too close to their entry price. They get immediately taken out in the noise.

Institutional Traders use Fibonacci Levels, Trend Lines, Simple S&R Levels to actually look for confluences to try and place their orders. They use [[Average True Range]] to determine S.L. Put your stop loss 2.5 times of ATR.
