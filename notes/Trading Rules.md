# Day Trading Rules
[...](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qL7Z7XQ6tPA)

## Plan it

- Do not get emotionally attached to any trade. Planning helps in preventing overtrading and FOMO (i.e. Fear On Missing Out).
- Always have a trading plan with entries, exits, and stops.
- Define risk levels for every single trade.
	- Say you see a stock for a potential long entry.
	- Then you only plan to buy this stock if it consolidates at a certain support levels with volume.
	- The stock has to have found a bottom support as a level for you to risk off.
	- Do not think you are limiting your profit by not picking stocks at the very bottom/top.
	- Waiting for confirmation and planning for entries with clear risk is going to make sure you profit from the meat of the move, and limit the risks at the same time.
- Beginners should have risk to reward of 1:3.
- Beginners should stay away from the small-cap penny stocks.
	- Typically small-cap stocks trade below $5. They are very illiquid and when volume does come in their price action will be very random.
	- Mid-cap and large-cap stocks are much more friendlier for new traders as their S/R levels are easier to plan out.
- Avoid trading market opens; its a hot mess.
	- Thousands of orders are getting filled during this time.
	- Its extremely volatile. Stops will easily get hit.
- During market open hours you should do the following:
	- Analyze the stock, the news and the price action.
	- Plan out the trade during this time.

### How to plan?

The plan should include following:
- Your thesis
- Risk to reward
- Your bias (Long or Short)
- All the important levels over or under
- Potential entries, exits and stops

```
$CLVS
- Extended daily chart
- Hot biotech sector
- 50% short interest
- Held PM gap and squeezed to new highs.

$CANF
- Nov 29 PM pump
- Old news
- Dilutive filings
- Bagholder chart
- Sold off entire PM gap
```

Trading is 90% planning and 10% execution.
- Spend 1 to 1.5 hours before the market opens in planning trade.
- Once the market opens spend remaining 10% of your time in executing your planned trades.

It is just as important to follow your plan as it is to make one.

If you don't follow your exit plan it can easily lead to th enext trading mistake.

## Track stock news

Your day trading account depends on the stock news so analyze stock news like your life depends on it.

- Analyze news around stocks you are planning to long/short especially if it made a significant move.
- Buying the dip is a good idea, but only in investing. This does not hold true for day trading.

## Stay away when emotional

Shutdown when feeling emotional. Market will always return back.

- Step away from trading station when emptions start kicking in.
- Do not get emptionally attached to any trade when you don't feel emotionally stable.
- Emotional trading leads to overtrading and even bigger losses.

## Be realistic

- Take the meat of the move and be realistic about trading profits.
- Be realistic about the stock's upside potential.
- Do not regret making small wins on a stock.
- Do not FOMO on missing out and selling too early. You can always buy back when when it dips.
- Do not aim for a home run. Focus on controlling your risk, and look for base hits. The profits are probably small, but taking these small wins are less stressful and they will add up to your account slowly.

In trading growth is exponential so whining about missing profits and jumping back into trades due to FOMO and the idea that "oh, i could have mmade so much more, this stock ownes me money now" is actually a very negative and dangerous emotion. Because eventually that will lead to new traders revisiting, and start revenge trading and giving back proofits.

When you are realistic about a trade it helps you to ignore those dollar signs, and becoming too attached to the money.

Why do most people get into trading?
Its mostly for the money. But ironically if you focus on just the money, you will fail in trading even before you take off.

If you are relatively new to the business, start trading with one or two stocks per day. Take the meat f the move and be done. Don't follow every single scanner alert and go for more stocks due to greed and FOMO (i.e. Fear Of Missing Out).

## Always prioritize risk management

- New traders ingore all risks and fail to see the potential downside if they are wrong.
- Without proper risk managment, it is going to be difficult to grow your account.
- Without setting stops and without setting max losses for the day, the trader is likely to, first, try to make back back small losses, then over trade, then start making mistakes, and eventually this is how many new traders blow up.
- Day trading is risky and thats why you should not be trading with the money you cannot afford to lose.
- New traders should not use margin buying power you cannot afford to lose.

Managing risk is curucial but you need to accept the fact that taking risks is necessary in trading. So you need to take calculated and planned risks and at the same time manage them so you won't go overboard.
