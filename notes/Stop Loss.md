# Stop Loss Reasons

Having an open trade is like having a child.

> A good child can turn bad but a bad child normally doesn't turn good.

If a child is not good from the start, they are not going to get better later on. If they are irritating at the beginning, they are going to remain irritating all their lives.

## Types of Trades

Once you have entered a trade forget all charting and just look at your entry point and its relationship with the current market. You will soon know what you have got in your hands; i.e., which one of the below 5 trades you have in your hands.

### A killer - The spoiled child

As soon you enter such trades, they never go in your favor. They do a complete nose dive and thats it. These are the kind of trades which normally kills your account.

At all costs you must avoid getting nailed down by such trades.

### A loser - The irritating child

As soon you enter such trades they slowly start to move against you but since they are slow you keep loyal to it. You think it should be OK as its its going slow  and then get stopped out.

### A lousy child

These are the trades that don't go anywhere.

### A slow winner - A good child

These are the trades that go mildly in your favor. People tend to cut these trades sooner than needed.

### A race horse - An ideal child

These are the trades that never go back to your entry point. They progress fast in your favor but unfortunately these are the very trades which people immediately cut as quickly as possible since you see profits sooner than imagined. People end up killing one of the best trades they have instead of riding it by putting trailing stop loss.

## Thought Process

If you open a trade which turns out to be `a lousy child` or anything less, think like so:

- How much money could this position make for you **in a day**.
- Say your estimation is 50, 60, 80 points but the problem is if you have lost 10 or 20 already you also want to additionally get those 10-20 back from that supposidly good trade.
- Now you must realize that your `ideal child` can cover all your losses, even 3 in a row and that too **in the same day**.

A position where there isn't an immediate pullback below the entry point is definitely worth holding on to.

> Takeoff and landing are the most dodgy parts of a flight.
 
If you have a good takeoff and you know the posistion didn't go hardly against you in the beginning then thats a very good indication that its a good position.

> One day you will eventually have a very big winner trade, a `race horse`, where your stop loss kept getting raised and raised and raised.

Once you start doing so you will realize how relatively easy the whole process. You can only have 1 of the 5 children in this game.

# The Action Plan

If you have to do anything in terms of spread betting its all about Stop Loss management. After you have opened a trade it doesn't really matter what the fundamentals or technicals are.

1. You take a position on the basis of a toss of a coin or charts.
2. After taking position you are not looking at the market anymore, you are just looking at your position once you have taken it.
3. You move your stop loss with that position until `The Market` stops you out.
4. Your greatest loss is in your initial stages only but once you can raise your stop loss, and keep raising it then you are normally going to be in a good health of your account.

> It will take a lot for you to wipe out an account if you simply kept raising your stop loss as the position moves in your favor.

## Goal #1 - Takeoff

Getting that `Stop Loss` upto the `Break Even` as quickly as you can is the name of the game.

> The secret is how long/quickly it takes for that position to get away from the battleground of your entry point.

![[Risk Management#S L Moving Strategy]]

You will quickly discover for your self that a position where a stop loss isn't hit in the first few minutes on a tight day trading position or there is no pullback below the entry point will tend to be a very big winner.

## Goal #2 - Fly & Land

Once your `Stop Loss` reaches the `Break Even` your next goal is to stay in that position as long as possible and ride the profit through trailing stop losses [[#Let the market stop you| until the market stops you out]].

# Stop Loss Strategies

> Always trade from the position of strength. Do not get wrecked!

## Let the market stop you

Its always better that market stops you rather than you choosing a number and getting stopped out by 1 point and then market going in your favor.

## Avoid being stop "hunted"
How to avoid being stop hunted?

[by UKSpreadBetting](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ur3AhuVnd1I)

### Putting tight stops

The tighter the stop loss, the more likely it will get triggerred!

> The Stop Loss should be placed at a point where the premise of the trade is negated if you get stopped.

Stop loss should only be set at a point where your strategy is completely invalidated. Say you have spotted a Double Top and you have put a S.L. above the Double Top level then if the Stop Loss does get hit it should only mean that the Double Top pattern was invalid. Stop Loss should not be at a point at which once hit, the price continues only confirm that your Double Top was correct.

### Putting stops at the same place as everybody else.

Market hunts for liquidity. They are not the cartel as such pinging your stops.

#### Put your stop far enough

The trouble with putting S.L. too far out of the way is the risk-reward getting skewed.

Example:
- [Live trade by Anish Singh Thakur](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dQ2jM5ATqIg)

#### Let other trader's S.L. get stopped out and then you get in.

Let the other retailers get stopped out first and then enter when price comes under your strategy, where it still is valid.

#### Enter where other people put their stops.

Put your Limit order for entering the trade at the very point where other retailers put stop loss.

Essentially you are taking advantage of other's S.L by entering with the S.L. while putting your own stop far away from others.

## Quit moving stops too soon

Stop moving your S.L. to `Break Even` or `Take Profit` too soon!

The beginning stages of a trade is important so don't move S.L. in the initial stages of the trade. Like a takeoff which is the most crucial part of a flight you must leave enough room for the price to takeoff.

Give price enough rooom to oscillate and absorb noise before it makes further gains. Let the time pass by and after that gradually moveup, but not immeidately after entering the trade.

[Anish Singh Thakur Live Trade for Trailing Stop Loss](https://youtu.be/dQ2jM5ATqIg?t=2072)
	- When the price is far away from the `Take Profit` level, do not move `Stop Loss`.
	- When the price has covered over 80% of its journey to `Take Profit` level only then start aggressive trailing the Stop Loss in order to protect your large gains so far.

Follow these approaches after entering a trade:

- To move S.L. consider using [[Indicators#Volatility Indicators]] like [[Indicators#Average True Range]] to determine next S.L. so that you leave enough room for your trade to breath before it makes new highs/lows.
- Wait for price to form new S&L level and then `move your S.L. to the new S&R level`. Think as if it was your new entry point.
- If you are really fearful about being stopped out, scale out of your posistion. `Exit some part of your trade` instead of moving S.L.
- You may choose to `move some part of your S.L.` instead of moving all of it.
- Example: [Anish Singh Thakur Live Trade](https://youtu.be/dQ2jM5ATqIg?t=2072)
### S.L. Moving Strategy

- As soon your position reaches 1 X of the S.L do as follows:
- Take out 25% of the profit
- Move S.L. to the `Break Even` point
- Optionally advace the `Take Profit` level from 1.5 times to 2 times of the original Stop Loss level.
