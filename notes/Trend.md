# Trend Phases

## Push Phase (Trend Continuation)

### Uptrend Continuation

- Price breaks above the previous structural high.
- Price pulls back to the keylevel for a retest.
- Price continues to the upside.

### Downtrend Continuation

- Price breaks below the previous structural low.
- Price pulls back to the keylevel for a retest.
- Price continues to the downside.

## Trendline
[...](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kmtWd3oTgTY)

Trendline ≈ [[Support & Resistance]]

where:
- S/R levels are horizontal lines
- Trendlines are sloped lines

### Trendline Rules

- Draw major trendlines which are obvious and clear.
- Only look at the major swing points for plotting trenlines.
- Ensure minimum 3 touches to confirm a trendline.
	- Try connecting maximum number of higher-lows (in an uptrend) or lower-highs (in a downtrend).
- Think of trendline as an area rather than a line.
	- Trendlines are areas of possible reversals so you may need 2 parallel lines to mark trendlines.
	- It will cover all the touches which may get left out by a single line.
	- Drawing 2 parallel lines will prevent scenarios where price reverses before touching the trendline or scenarios where price breaches the trendline and then reverses.
- For trendlines on a larger dispersed dataset use `Logarithmic Charts` instead of Arithmatic/Linear charts.
	- A a very good trend may completely get missed out by an `Aritmatic/Linear Chart` as it will show it as a choppy price movement.
	- Logarithmic scale correctly depicts the percent change.
		- In logarithmic scale the distance between ₹10 and ₹20 will be same as that between ₹100 and ₹200.
		- In arithmatic/linear scale distance betwen ₹10 and ₹20 will be 10 units whereas distance between ₹100 and ₹200 will be 100 units.
	- Trendlines are best represented using percent change.

## Trading Trendlines

The further the trend goes the lesser the probability you have of getting paid.

### Trading Trendline Reversals

`Trend-Following Strategy:` In this strategy we catchup moves in an uptrend and catch down moves in a downtrend i.e. we enter trends in pullbacks. [...](https://youtu.be/kmtWd3oTgTY?t=234)

#### Trendlines with S/R levels

- Identify the formation of HH-HL/LH-LL market structure. 
- Plot the trendline.
- Plot [[Support & Resistance]].
- Wait for the [[Pullback]] near the `areas of high confluence` between trendline and S/R level.
	- The points where trendline and S/R line conincides are the areas where a trade w/ high probability of success can be planned.
- Wait for the candlestick pattern to trigger an entry.
- Enter trade with R/R = 1:2.

### Trading Trendline Breakouts

`Trendline Breakout Strategy:` When the price is trending in one direction it will never break its trendline but when the price does break the trendline it can indicate a strong reversal. If we catch the breakout of the trendline we can ride the next trend by entering in its early stage. [...](https://youtu.be/kmtWd3oTgTY?t=440)

#### Avoiding Fakeouts

The price may break the trendline just to reverse and resume the existing trend again. This type of price movement is called fakeout.

To avoid fakeouts use [[Indicators#Volume Indicators]]

- We look at the size of volume bars in relation to the previous bars.
- If there is a spike in volume around the breakout of trendline then we have a high probability of a trend reversal.
- Enter the trade at the close of candle which broke the trendline.
- We trade with R/R = 1:2
