## Overtrading

### Do not overtrade

- Most beginners focus on trading profits (P&L) instead of trading the plan and following the chart patterns.
	- Lets say on a given day you made $800 by trading here and there.
	- As your whole focus is on making P&L look good, your goal becomes to round that $800 to $1000.
	- So instead of taking a break during the middle of the day, you keep on trading.
	- As a result you start entering trades that perhaps are not your A+ setup. Due to this you start losing.
	- Your $800 becomes $650, $650 becomes $375 and so on.
	- You get emotional and extremely frustrated. You not only don't have $1000, you also lost your $800 profit you just had half an hour ago.
- Overtrading leads to emotional trading and emotional trading leads to revenge trading. You keep going back to the stock you've just lost on and attempt to get that money back.
- You may have learned the skills to make money in the market but you may not have the skills to keep it because you let the greed and emotion get in the way by overtrading.

### Avoid overtrading like so:

- Follow your stop loss. No execuses there. If you planned on getting out of that trade at $8, do it. Yes, it sucks to take a loss but remember it is a planned loss that is within your risk profile as part of your trading plan.
- Walk away from your computer during the lunch hours (i.e. 11:30 to 2:30 PM EST). Generally during lunch hours stocks are consolidating in a tight range with low trading volume; not much action happens.

## Scaling up too quickly

Scaling up position size too quickly leads to emotional trading.

Do not underestimate all the emotional stress that comes with scaling up so quickly.
- If your earlier risk was losing $30 to make $100 per day, by scaling 10X it would immediately become losing $300 to make $1000. Although you may be ready to earn that $1000 but you may not be ready to handle the $300 loss every day.
- When you scale your position size too quickly, it will lead to emotional trading.

Scale up slowly.
Take your time with sizing.
Give it a couple of months before adding a quarter each time and doubling it eventually.
Taking it slow will allow you to mentally prepare for the possible bigger proportional losses.
