# Step 1. Find a bullish/bearish pressure zones.
[...](https://youtu.be/OKPgwonsnFU?t=99)

The location of the pressure zones in the market is the most important factor to consider when trading. [...](https://youtu.be/OKPgwonsnFU?t=790)

Look for periods when momentum is fading away, and a potential trend change is on the way.

In a Heiken-ashi chart, trend changes can be identified by looking at the shape, size and direction of the candlesticks.

in an uptrend [...](https://youtu.be/OKPgwonsnFU?t=115)
- If the candlestick starts becoming smaller and more evenly sized then it indicates that the uptrend is losing momentum and the trend change is eminent.
- Consecutive upper wicks in an uptrend indicates bearish pressure and consecutive lower wicks in a downtrend indicates bullish pressure.
- Wicks indicates potential buying/selling pressure.
- When the candle shadows overlap, they create a price zone that is more likely to exhibit a buying/selling pressure you anticipate.
- Small shadows can create unreliable pressure zones. The wicks of these candles must be larger in size to indicate a pressure zone. [...](https://youtu.be/OKPgwonsnFU?t=218)

A strong Heikin-ashi pressure zone is not defined just by one candle with a big wick.

A single price bar might be sufficiently reliable for a trading setup.

To filter false signal, look for a group of candlesticks (at least 2 consecutive bars, more even better) with overlapping upper/lower shadows.

For potential trend change:
	- Consecutive heikin ashi bars with overlapping lower shadows create a [bullish pressure zone](https://youtu.be/OKPgwonsnFU?t=198)
	- Consecutive heikin ashi bars with overlapping upper shadows create a [bearish pressure zone](https://youtu.be/OKPgwonsnFU?t=239)

### Confluence 1. Use Wicks
[...](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OKPgwonsnFU)
##### Anticipating momentum shift

Anticipate bullish pressure in a continued downtrend by spotting 3 consecutive lower wicks.
##### Anticipating volatility/indecision

- Large wicks indicate high volatility.
	- If high and low price are significantly different from the open and close price then it indicates that a lot of buying and selling activity is taking place and there is a lack of clear direction.
	- A series of candlesticks with long wicks indicate a potential trend change
- Small wicks or absence of it indicate market is stable

### Confluence 2. Use previous S/L levels
[...](https://youtu.be/OKPgwonsnFU?t=257)

Heikin ashi wicks should not be used in isolation.

Carefully consider the market context, when trying to identify the potential trend reversals or changes in market sentiment.

### Confluence 3. Use VWAP
[...](https://youtu.be/OKPgwonsnFU?t=460)

Trade bullish/bearish heiken ashi zones w/ weekly VWAP.

Weekly VWAP offer dynamic S/L levels.

This strategy puts less emphasis on the length of a single shadow and instead focuses on multiple shadows.

##### Find pressure zones around VWAP
[...](https://youtu.be/OKPgwonsnFU?t=512)

- Find bullish pressure zones (tripple lower wicks) above the weekly VWAP. This is a buy/demand zone.
- Find bearish pressure zones (tripple upper wicks) below the weekly VWAP. This is a sell/supply zone.

### Confluence 4. Use Fib retracement
[...](https://youtu.be/OKPgwonsnFU?t=595)

Our entry levels should also align with 50% fib retracement levels. 

### Confluence 5. Psychological numbers
[...](https://youtu.be/OKPgwonsnFU?t=608)

Heikin ashi pressure zones work extremely well in combination with psychological numbers.

Price tends to stall at certain numbers that ends with 10, 50 or 100 because they are often used as reference points (like for choosing strike price etc). Operators/traders put orders or set limits on such round numbers.

# Step 2. Wait for the setup

Look at a higher timeframe to get a sense of overall direction of the market and identify the pressure zones that may not be visible on shorter time frames. [...](https://youtu.be/OKPgwonsnFU?t=709)
- HTF helps in filtering out noise in shorter time frames.
- HTF has fewer price fluctuations.
- Its easier to identify major S/L levels.
- Consecutive wicks on LTF is quite common but in HTF it has more relevance.
- In HTF, psychological numbers are more obvious. The more zeroes the number has the stronger the psychological level is.

### Approach 1: With 10 & 30 EMAs
[...](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p7ZYrxZo_38)

#### Bullish View
[...](https://youtu.be/p7ZYrxZo_38?t=472)

1. The higher timeframe must be in uptrend making HH and HL.
2. The 10 EMA is above the 30 EMA
3. The trading timeframe should satisfy both criteria 1 and 2 as well.

#### Short View
[...](https://youtu.be/p7ZYrxZo_38?t=556)

1. The higher timeframe must be in downtrend making LL and LH.
2. The 10 EMA is below the 30 EMA
3. The trading timeframe should satisfy both criteria 1 and 2 as well.

### Approach 2: With 9, 21, 55 EMAs

#### Bullish View

1. The recent highest swing high must be broken by the Heiken Ashi candle.
2. Wait for 9 EMA to cross above the 55 EMA.
3. Wait for 21 EMA to cross above the 55 EMA.
4. Buy at the close of the candlestick following the moving average crossover.
	1. Before buying watch for the previous swing high on the left side.
	2. We should only buy from the close of the candlestick that breaks the previous swing high.
5. Put SL at a few pips below the low of the entry candle.

#### Short View
[...](https://youtu.be/nYrXM1h4QCk?t=491)

1. Pay attention to the recent lowest swing low of the range because it must be broken by the Heiken Ashi candle to consider opening a short position.
2. Wait for 9 EMA to cross below the 55 EMA.
3. Wait for 21 EMA to cross below the 55 EMA.
4. Sell at the close of the candlestick following the moving average crossover.
	1. Before selling watch for the previous swing low on the left side.
	2. We should only buy from the close of the candlestick that breaks the previous swing low.
5. Put SL at a few pips above the high of the entry candle.

### Approach 1: With 10 & 30 EMAs
[...](https://youtu.be/p7ZYrxZo_38?t=536)

Look to go long when
- Price bounces off either of the 2 EMAs.
- First `bullish heiken ashi candle` is formed with a `flat bottom` and `higher low`.

Look to go short when
- Price bounces off either of the 2 EMAs.
- First `bearish heiken ashi candle` is formed with a `flat top` and a `lower high`.
- Go short at the close of this bar.


# Step 4. Put Stop Loss
[...](https://youtu.be/OKPgwonsnFU)

For a short trade put stop loss is above the bearish pressure zone (i.e. above the 3 consecutive upper wicks).

For a long trade put stop loss below the bullish pressure zone (i.e. below the 3 consecutive lower wicks).
