Developing a solid mechanical trade strategy is critical for success in any size trading account.

# Step 1. Develop a logic chain

The most important first step is to develop a well thought out logic chain.
- Logic chain has nothing to do with what is ultimately going to happen with this underlying.
- Logic chain is about making sure that all the pieces line up and you can connect all the dots w.r.t. whatever your assumption is and whatever strategy you are going to apply.

# Step 2. Create a watchlist

Create a watch list of 30-50 stocks to follow is an important first step.
- They should be liquid so you can rely on pricing efficiency. Liquidity on a smaller account has a greater percentage effect than it does on the larger account.
- By limiting the number you can become comfortable and familiar with them to aid in trade assumptions.
	- Rather than constantly watching your PnL, watch and track your watchlist.

On your watchlist keep an eye on IV and IV percentile to aid in trade decisions.
- Ask following key questions to have a reasonable expectations about expected move, the direction, and the price effeciency:
	- Is IV high or low relative to the market?
	- Where does IV fall in its percentile range?

# Step 3. Make mean reversion assumptions

Look at the price to see if there are any mean reversion assumptions that can be made?

- Ensure IV percentile is either high or atleast far above average.
- As an option writer you sell IV because you want its reversion to the mean.
- Option writer love to sell premium above historical averages because when volatility falls, and your starting point is above the number that you expected to get down to its good.
- When you are selling IV that means you are expecting a certain type of move. You are not likely to get that move if you are facing complacency or capitulation because of volatility extreme.
	- You are not going to get a price advantage if you are situated at a volatility level that you are not going to get paid off.
	- So if the market moves in a direction that might not be the direction you want to go as long as volatility is contracting you can still make money.
- Shorting IV above the mean is a lot more reliable than shorting below the mean.
- When you short you are essentially betting on the contraction in volatility.
- IV is mean reverting. If IV percentile is low at the time of trade then you not only collect lesser premium but you also risk from higher volatility later which will make your premium expensive in future thereby resulting in losses.

# Step 4. Make trading assumptions

Look to create your trading assumptions:

- Are you Bullish? Bearish? Neutral?
- Are you looking for `price reversion`?
	- It means whether the underlying is going `overbought or oversold`.
	- Don't think of price reversion to the mean very much when it comes to trading stocks.
	- IV reversion still very much applies to stocks.
- Is `IV reverting` to the mean?
	- It means you sell when IV is high? You don't buy when IV is cheap.
	- EITHER you have an overbought/oversold condition with IV at 100% percentile.
	- OR you can have an overbought/oversold condition with IV at its bottom end.
- Possible [[Glossary#Pairs Trade | pairs trade]] candidate?
	- Buying bonds and selling S&Ps or vice-versa.

# Step 5. Pick a strategy

Choose a trading strategy based on:
- Overall IV of the security and the market.
	- If market is trading at 100% and security is trading at 70% then you will trade ETF.
- Look for defined risk trades (ex. vertical credit spreads, iron condors)
	- Defining risk is the starting point.
	- With experience its fine entering trade w/o defined risk trades.
	- With smaller accounts you want to be careful that you have enough capital left over for strategic diversification.
- Does the strategy effectively capture the right amount of premium (ex. 1/3 width of strike, high PoP)

# Step 6. Execution/Exit strategy
[Criteria for entering an options trade](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=diskN74_paQ)

## Trade Size

- Trade small size.
- Management of risk after the trade has been placed is a common fallacy.
- The only time to do this is on order entry. Do this by staying small.
- Whatever you do, just stay small.

## Trade Time

- On average, volume is greater on the open an dclose. Its that these times we see less slippage and quicker fills. However, recognizing when an underlying is experiencing moments of illiquidity is critical to the timing of the in order entry process. Overall, these days time of the day does not matter.

## DTE

- Have enough time in trade that allows you to be right. Roughly 45 DTE allows you to collect decent premium while affording th eflexibilty to let the timework in your favor.
- It does not mean that 20 DTE or 55 DTE doesn't work, its just that 45 DTE gives the highest RoC (Return on Capital).
- There is a 150% RoC difference in the 45 DTE slot than it does in 3 weeks slot w/ 20-25 DTE or > 55 DTE.

#### Nuances

- Choose a price point for entry and exits, avoid "chasing trades".
	- Look for some price event
- Manage winners (defend losers)
- Employ profit or date based exit strategy for closing our or rolling trades.
