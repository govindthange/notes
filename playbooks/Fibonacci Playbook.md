# Step 1. Plot the fibonacci levels on higher timeframe

1. Go the a higher timeframe (say Weekly)
2. Plot the fibonacci levels
	- In an uptrend, start from the previous swing-low to the previous swing high.
	- In a downtrend, start from the previous swing-high to the previous swing-low.

# Step 2. Mark the key retracement levels

1. Mark the 0.5 and 0.618 levels as the key retracement levels.
	- Levels below 0.382 are not reliable.
	- 0.382 is too aggressive and should be traded with less capital.
		- At this level the S.L needs to be at 1.0 as price can retrace to 1.0.
		- If you enter at this level and price retraces further you have to handle emotions.
	- 0.786 and 1.0 are deep, less likely but great to have retracements.
		- S.L at 1.0 offers less risk to reward ratio.
	- If price goes beyond 1.0 then it is a sign of reversal.

# Step 3. Track the price action

1. Observe the price action on the higher timeframe
	- The price would generally come down to test 0.5 level.
	- The market respects 0.5 level more than the 0.618.
	- The price may go and test 0.618 or it may just wick to that level.
2. Track the price action on lower timeframe.
	- Observe how price is creating S/R levels at the key fibonacci levels.
	- Analyze wicks and candlestick patterns around the key fibonacci levels.

# Step 4. Enter the trade on 2nd test

- Let the price retrace to the key fibonacci level.
- Let the price bounce back.
- Let the price retest the key level the 2nd time.
- Enter the trade on 2nd bounce.

# Step 5. Exit the trade

- Exit at -0.23, -0.75, and -1.2. Note that these are different from fibonacci extension.
- Exit at 1.0 stop loss.
